## Final editorial decisions

- Red-kangaroo wording asks for its native country rather than treating a continent label as a zoological fact.
- The Indian Ocean clue names Bunbury, Western Australia, matching the opened Australian Government location evidence. This is a family-friendly easy geography item; the other five easy geography rows are the deliberately child-targeted subset.
- General calendar wording asks for exactly 28 days in a non-leap year; it does not use the ambiguous wording that all months have at least 28 days.
- The pedestrian signal question is scoped to Australia. The Ural Mountains question specifies the north–south range through western Russia so that the Caucasus is excluded.
- The bowline clue includes the familiar rabbit/hole/tree teaching mnemonic, since many knots can make a fixed loop.
- Allspice keeps no aliases: pimento can also mean a different pepper, so it is unnecessary here.
- All facts are stable and have no expiry. The Sumatra item asks about native range rather than a live population count or record. Monetary-unit wording does not ask a volatile exchange rate.
- Kids-targeted easy subsets: science 7 (life cycle, body, gravity, roots, familiar planet, fish, snakes); geography 5 (Australia, Canada, South America, Equator, England); general 6 (shapes, time, fruit, utensils, road crossing, calendar). Each category also has further familiar school/family subjects at medium: science pollination and helium, geography peninsula, general hummus and acronyms. Kids suitability and difficulty are editorial estimates for ages 8–12 and educated Australian adults respectively.
