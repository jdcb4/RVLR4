A little more game.

Three artwork directions for RVLRY. Same compact screens, different character. Try them on the picker, a handoff, a live turn and the finish.

Proposal only · no app changes · works offline

My recommendation: larger illustrations on the picker, handoffs and results; a small game mark during timed play. All three can live with the existing themes. Trivia artwork is exploratory—the game is not built.

A · Paper personalities

Bright screenprint illustrations with a cut-sticker edge. Closest to the spirit of Recess.

Why: instant recognition and a playful silhouette even at small sizes. Trade-off: the bright palette has more presence in quieter themes.

B · Tabletop pieces

Miniature painted game objects. Warm, tactile and familiar without feeling like a kids-only app.

Why: a strong shared identity across all five themes. Trade-off: needs consistent lighting and scale as the asset family grows. My first choice.

C · Cabinet of curiosities

Brass, enamel and engraved detail. More like opening a beautifully illustrated game box.

Why: rich character for Observatory, the pub and book-club themes. Trade-off: detail becomes less distinct at small sizes; heavier than A or B.

What would actually change?

More personality, in the useful places

  • Picker: game artwork stays left of the title, vertically centred.
  • Handoff: a larger game illustration makes a natural pause between turns, with the active player and action still dominant.
  • Results: a small celebratory arrival around the winning team, alongside scores and fun facts.

Keep the play area clear

  • During play: small game mark only. The word, timer, recent words and thumb controls keep their space.
  • Controls: Home, Settings, Info and other actions stay Lucide icons.
  • Production pass after your choice: refine edges and small-size legibility, create matching result accents and optimise local assets. These generated concept sheets are not final production artwork.

Trivia: the decisions before the plan

One presenter, one phone. Choose a question count and categories; draw from a reviewed offline bank. “Generate a quiz” means selecting questions—not calling AI while people are playing.

Recommended first version

  • Answers off-phone: people remember or write on paper. Presenter reveals after each question or together at the end. Decide whether in-app answer entry is worth adding later.
  • No automatic marking: show the answer and acceptable variants. Decide whether a simple manual team scoreboard is wanted for v1.
  • Question count: 10, 20, 30 or a custom count, limited to available questions. Mixed categories are balanced; no duplicates within a quiz.

Content and reliability

  • Categories: General Knowledge; History; Geography; Science & Nature; Arts & Literature; Film & TV; Music; Sport & Games. Map old categories during review.
  • Question record: question, answer/variants, category, difficulty, kids suitability, source/review details and optional expiry. Start with ordinary spoken-answer questions.
  • Expiry and resume: expired questions are not presented, including after resuming. Save quiz order, progress and reveal state so interruptions do not expose answers or reshuffle the quiz.
  • Ingestion first: preserve original source → normalise and deduplicate → check facts, age fit and expiry → review → publish a versioned offline pack. Nothing found is approved automatically.
